# Create Legal Problem Scenarios with ChatGPT 5.4 for Sydney Law School

Hypothetical scenarios are central to legal problem-solving activities. This guide updates the supplied ChatGPT4o/o3-mini workflow for the current **ChatGPT 5.4 for Sydney Law School** agent.

## Step 1: State the purpose

Frame the task clearly.

```
I am a legal educator creating a hypothetical scenario for a law school problem question.
```

## Step 2: Specify the area of law

Identify the legal domain and, where useful, the particular doctrines students should need to recognise or apply.

```
The scenario should relate to tort law, specifically nuisance and trespass.
```

## Step 3: Set the teaching or assessment context

Explain how the scenario will be used, for example in a tutorial, in-class activity, formative quiz or assessment exercise.

```
It will be used in a tutorial to help students identify relevant legal issues and apply IRAC.
```

## Step 4: Set the desired complexity

Indicate the number of parties, issues and level of ambiguity.

```
The scenario should include at least two identifiable legal issues and involve at least two parties.
```

## Step 5: Keep it realistic and focused

Ask for a plausible legal fact pattern rather than an unnecessarily dramatic narrative.

```
Keep the facts realistic and suitable for educational purposes. Avoid extreme or sensationalised events and include only details that contribute to the legal analysis.
```

## Step 6: Add follow-up questions if useful

```
After the scenario, include one or two questions asking students to identify legal issues or apply relevant legal principles.
```

## Combined prompt template

```
I am a legal educator creating a hypothetical scenario for a law school problem question.

The scenario should relate to [area of law].

It will be used in a [tutorial/class/formative activity/assessment] to help students [identify issues/apply IRAC/evaluate competing arguments].

The scenario should:
1. include at least [number] legal issues;
2. involve at least [number] parties;
3. use realistic, professionally plausible facts;
4. include only facts that contribute to the intended legal analysis; and
5. be suitable for [undergraduate/postgraduate] law students.

After the scenario, include one or two questions asking students to identify or apply relevant legal principles.
```

## Undergraduate and postgraduate calibration

### Undergraduate

- **Issue complexity:** usually one or two relatively identifiable issues.
- **Context:** familiar or local factual settings can be useful.
- **Language:** clear and accessible.
- **Question guidance:** more specific prompts can scaffold issue spotting and application.
- **Typical focus:** identification of issues and application of established principles.

### Postgraduate

- **Issue complexity:** multiple, potentially interrelated or ambiguous issues.
- **Context:** professionally framed scenarios such as client advice, memoranda or complex transactions.
- **Language:** professional and appropriately nuanced.
- **Question guidance:** more open-ended prompts may be suitable.
- **Typical focus:** advanced reasoning, competing arguments, policy implications or critique of doctrine.

## Review before use

Check the generated scenario for doctrinal accuracy, realism, unintended ambiguity, unnecessary factual cues and alignment with the intended learning outcome. Verify any embedded legal propositions against authoritative sources.
