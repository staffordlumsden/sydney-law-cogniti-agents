Build a Rubric with ChatGPT 5.4 for Sydney Law School

This guide updates the supplied ChatGPT4o rubric workflow for the current ChatGPT 5.4 for Sydney Law School agent. The underlying prompting sequence remains useful: establish the educational context, describe the task, specify the rubric structure, define the assessment focus and require a concise output.

Step 1: State the educator context

Tell the agent who you are and what you are designing.

I am a legal educator designing a rubric for a law school assessment task.

Step 2: Describe the assessment task

Specify what students must produce, including relevant format, word length, level and subject matter.

The task requires students to write a 3,000-word legal research essay on discrimination law.

Useful details include:

  • task type;
  • word or time limit;
  • year level or award level;
  • relevant area of law;
  • Unit Learning Outcomes or capabilities being assessed; and
  • any mandatory task requirements.

Step 3: Specify the number of criteria

State the approximate number of criteria you want. The supplied workflow recommends keeping the rubric manageable rather than proliferating criteria.

Please generate a simple, clear rubric with four criteria.

Step 4: Specify format, tone and audience

Ask for descriptors that students and markers can use readily.

Each criterion should include a concise descriptor suitable for postgraduate law students. Use plain English and an academic but accessible tone.

Step 5: Define the assessment focus

Use the task requirements and learning outcomes to specify what the rubric should assess.

Common areas include:

  • legal research and referencing;
  • critical analysis;
  • legal reasoning and argumentation;
  • use of case law and legislation;
  • structure and coherence; and
  • written expression.

Criteria should reflect legal research skills, legal reasoning, structure and coherence, and use of legal authority.

Step 6: Specify the output

Tell the agent to keep the rubric concise and provide it in a practical format.

Keep the rubric concise for easy marking and student understanding. Present it as a table.

Combined prompt

I am a legal educator designing a rubric for a law school assessment task.

The task requires students to write a 3,000-word legal research essay on discrimination law.

Please generate a clear rubric with four criteria. Each criterion should include a concise descriptor suitable for postgraduate law students. Use plain English and keep the tone academic but accessible.

The criteria should reflect:
- legal research skills;
- legal reasoning;
- structure and coherence; and
- use of legal authority.

Keep the rubric concise for easy marking and student understanding. Present it as a table.

Before adopting the output

Check that the proposed criteria:

  • assess what the task actually requires students to demonstrate;
  • align with the relevant Unit Learning Outcomes;
  • are sufficiently distinct from one another;
  • use observable language;
  • are proportionate to the weighting and complexity of the task; and
  • can be applied consistently by markers.

The agent should support rubric development, not determine the final assessment standard.
