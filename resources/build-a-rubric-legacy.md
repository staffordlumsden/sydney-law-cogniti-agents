## **🧭 Creating a Rubric Prompt Using ChatGPT4o for SLS**

### **🔹 Step 1: Start with the Educator Context**

Begin your prompt by explaining your role. This helps the AI understand the perspective and tone it should adopt.

**Example**:

> I am a legal educator designing a rubric for a law school assessment task.
> 

### **🔹 Step 2: Briefly Describe the Assessment Task**

Clearly describe what students are expected to do. Include the format, word count, and content focus.

**Prompt Template**:

> The task requires students to [briefly describe task, e.g., write a 3,000-word legal research essay on discrimination law].
> 

**✅ Tips**:

- Use plain language.
- Focus on what the student must produce.
- Mention any key topics or laws involved.

### **🔹 Step 3: Specify the Number of Criteria**

Indicate how many rubric criteria you want—typically 3 to 5. More than 5 can be overwhelming for both marking and students.

**Prompt Example**:

> Please generate a simple, clear rubric that includes 3–5 criteria.
> 

### **🔹 Step 4: Clarify the Format and Tone**

Tell ChatGPT4o to keep descriptors short, in plain English, and accessible to students. Specify academic tone but avoid jargon.

**Prompt Example**:

> Each criterion should include a short descriptor and be suitable for undergraduate/postgraduate [choose one] law students.
> 

**✅ Tip**: Choose the level based on your class (e.g., undergraduate or postgraduate).

### **🔹 Step 5: Define the Focus of Assessment**

Help the AI understand what the rubric should assess. Use learning outcomes, skills, or knowledge areas.

**Prompt Example**:

> Criteria should reflect [insert relevant learning outcomes or focus areas, e.g., legal reasoning, structure and clarity, use of authority].
> 

**✅ Tip**: Common focus areas include:

- Legal research and referencing
- Critical analysis
- Argumentation
- Use of case law/statutes
- Written expression

### **🔹 Step 6: Ask for a Concise Output**

Let the AI know this is a practical tool—not an academic treatise.

**Prompt Example**:

> Keep the rubric concise for easy marking and student understanding.
> 

And tell it how you would like the output to look like

**Prompt Example**:

> answer in the form of a table.
> 

## **🧩 Final Prompt Template**

Here’s what it all looks like when assembled:

```
I am a legal educator designing a rubric for a law school assessment
task. The task requires students to write a 3,000-word legal research
essay on discrimination law. Please generate a simple, clear rubric that
includes 4 criteria. Each criterion should include a short descriptor
and be suitable for postgraduate law students. Use plain English and
keep the tone academic but accessible. Criteria should reflect legal
research skills, legal reasoning, structure and coherence, and use of
legal authority. Keep the rubric concise for easy marking and student
understanding.

```

Copy and paste the prompt above and customise it to your unit, or use the filled in version below
