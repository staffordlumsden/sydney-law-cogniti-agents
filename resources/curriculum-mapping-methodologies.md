Mapping Methodologies
How assessments address Unit Learning Outcomes (ULOs)
The qualitative mapping process is central to Step 2 (Mapping Assessment Tasks to ULOs) and informs subsequent steps, including Step 3 
(Justification of Alignment) and Step 4 (Validation Against Learning Frameworks). By systematically evaluating how each assessment addresses 
the ULOs, the process ensures that the unit’s assessments are aligned with its intended learning outcomes and broader educational standards.
1. Analysis of Assessment Tasks
Each assessment task is carefully reviewed to identify:
• Purpose: What the task is designed to assess (e.g., knowledge, skills, application, communication, ethical reasoning).
• Nature of the Task: Whether it involves foundational knowledge, practical application, critical analysis, or communication.
• Expected Outputs: What students are required to produce (e.g., answers to multiple-choice questions, preparation notes, practical 
submissions, or written exam responses).
Assessment Type: Whether the task is formative (e.g., quizzes) or summative (e.g., final exam), and whether it focuses on individual or 
collaborative work.
2. Mapping Assessment Tasks to ULOs
Each assessment task is mapped to the ULOs by:
• Identifying Relevant ULOs: Determining which ULOs align 
with the skills, knowledge, or competencies being assessed in 
the task.
• Matching Task Requirements to ULO Verbs: Comparing the 
verbs and actions in the ULOs (e.g., "analyse," "evaluate," 
"communicate") with the cognitive and practical demands of 
the assessment task.
• Evaluating Task Scope: Considering whether the task fully or 
partially addresses the ULOs based on its complexity and 
focus.
Scoring Alignment Between Assessments and ULOs
A numerical scale is often used to evaluate the extent to which each 
assessment addresses each ULO. For example:
2 = Fully addresses the ULO: The assessment directly and 
comprehensively evaluates the knowledge, skills, or attributes 
described in the ULO.
1 = Partially addresses the ULO: The assessment indirectly or 
partially evaluates the ULO, or only addresses some aspects of it.
0 = Does not address the ULO: The assessment does not evaluate 
the ULO at all.
Each assessment is scored against each ULO using this scale.

3. Specific Adjustments for Each Process
Mapping ULOs to CLOs
• Disciplinary Focus: The mapping focuses on how the ULOs contribute to the specific knowledge, skills, and attributes required for the LLB 
program.
• Program-Level Outcomes: The ULOs are mapped to CLOs that reflect the overarching goals of the LLB program, such as understanding 
legal principles, critical thinking, ethical reasoning, and cultural competence.
• Validation Against CALD TLOs: The alignment is cross-checked against the Council of Australian Law Deans (CALD) Threshold Learning 
Outcomes, which are specific to legal education.
Mapping ULOs to Graduate Qualities
• Broader Focus: The mapping focuses on how the ULOs contribute to transferable skills and attributes that apply across all disciplines, 
such as critical thinking, communication, and cultural competence.
• Graduate Attributes: The ULOs are mapped to the University of Sydney Graduate Qualities, which are broader and not discipline-specific.

4. When and Where the Qualitative Mapping Occurs
• For ULOs to CLOs: The qualitative mapping occurs during the program-level review of the unit, ensuring that the unit contributes 
meaningfully to the LLB program outcomes. This is typically done in collaboration with program coordinators or curriculum committees.
• For ULOs to Graduate Qualities: The qualitative mapping occurs during the university-wide review of the unit, ensuring that the unit 
supports the broader graduate attributes expected of all students. This is often part of the university’s quality assurance processes.
5. Summary
While the processes for mapping ULOs to CLOs and ULOs to Graduate Qualities share a common structure (e.g., qualitative mapping, justification, 
validation), they differ in focus and scope. Mapping ULOs to CLOs is more discipline-specific, focusing on the legal knowledge and skills required for 
the LLB program, while mapping ULOs to Graduate Qualities is more general, focusing on transferable skills and attributes applicable across all 
disciplines. Both processes ensure that the unit aligns with its intended outcomes and contributes meaningfully to the broader educational goals of 
the program and the university.

Simplified example of how Python might be used to map ULOs to CLOs:
from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import TfidfVectorizer
from sklearn.metrics.pairwise import cosine_similarity
# Example ULOs and CLOs
ulos = [
 "Analyse and apply the principles of open justice and case management.",
 "Evaluate and implement appropriate Civil Dispute Resolution procedures."
]
clos = [
 "Demonstrate an integrated understanding of legal principles.",
 "Apply critical thinking to legal questions and problems."
]
# Vectorise the text
vectorizer = TfidfVectorizer()
vectors = vectorizer.fit_transform(ulos + clos)
# Calculate similarity
similarity_matrix = cosine_similarity(vectors[:len(ulos)], vectors[len(ulos):])
# Display results
for i, ulo in enumerate(ulos):
 print(f"ULO: {ulo}")
 for j, clo in enumerate(clos):
 print(f" CLO: {clo} - Similarity: {similarity_matrix[i][j]:.2f}")
