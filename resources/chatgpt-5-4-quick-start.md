# Quick Start: ChatGPT 5.4 for Sydney Law School

**Current iteration:** ChatGPT 5.4 for Sydney Law School  
**Audience:** Sydney Law School academic and professional staff  
**Platform:** Cogniti

ChatGPT 5.4 for Sydney Law School is the current general-purpose Cogniti workspace for low-risk teaching, curriculum-development and administrative tasks. The supplied earlier guide referred to ChatGPT4o; this version updates the model name and removes model-specific claims that are no longer current.

## Getting started

1. Open **ChatGPT 5.4 for Sydney Law School** from the Cogniti Agent Hub.
2. If prompted, select **Login with Microsoft**.
3. Sign in with your University credentials and complete Okta verification.
4. No installation or local technical setup is required.

## What it is useful for

Use the agent to accelerate first drafts and structured thinking for tasks such as:

- drafting or refining rubrics and marking guides;
- developing tutorial questions and legal hypotheticals;
- generating formative activities;
- producing plain-English explanations of legal concepts;
- restructuring instructional content;
- preparing quiz content for Canvas;
- structuring feedback or policy summaries.

The agent is deliberately general purpose. Give it the context, constraints and source material needed for the task, then review and refine the output using your professional judgement.

## Example prompts

> Create a four-criterion rubric for a 3,000-word legal essay on contract law for second-year undergraduate students. Use plain English and align each criterion to the task requirements.

> Give me three plain-English explanations of estoppel suitable for teaching first-year law students. Flag any legal propositions that I should verify against an authoritative source.

> Draft five tutorial questions based on a realistic workplace discrimination hypothetical. The questions should move from issue identification to application and evaluation.

## Good practice

- Keep prompts specific about audience, purpose, task and desired output.
- Provide authoritative source material when accuracy depends on a particular policy, legal authority or curriculum document.
- Treat generated material as a draft rather than a finished product.
- Review and edit outputs before sharing them with students.
- Verify legal, policy and scholarly claims against authoritative sources.
- Do not enter personal, protected or highly protected data where the model is not deployed in an Australian geographic region.

## Important limitation

ChatGPT 5.4 for Sydney Law School is **not a search engine** and should not be treated as an authoritative source. It does not replace academic judgement, legal research, curriculum governance or quality assurance.
